# Red-team evaluation: escalation and policy simplification

Checked 17 October 2026. Methodology recommendation for [Challenge whether the experiment can prove escalation and policy simplification](https://github.com/awjreynolds/p4ai/issues/12); no compiler experiment or model call was performed.

**Verdict: conditional proceed, with a narrower initial claim.** Test replacement of one named NVIDIA scheduling selector, then separately test maintenance and adaptation. Do not claim compiler-wide simplification, novel learned heuristics, or general target portability from that result. LLVM MLGO already replaces inlining and register-allocation heuristics with learned models while separating correctness constraints from optimization decisions. [LLVM MLGO](https://llvm.org/docs/MLGO.html)

**Strongest objection:** deliberately deleting useful selection logic creates a recovery task, not evidence that escalation makes a production compiler simpler. Existing policy may still choose the candidate set, encode the answer in features or prompts, reject alternatives, or rescue failures through fallback. Matching production performance can therefore coexist with increased total maintenance.

## Make the intervention falsifiable

Before implementation, identify the precise selector, its callers, admissible actions, target assumptions, and downstream decisions. Inventory policy retained, removed, and introduced across code, tables, candidate generation, feature extraction, prompts, retrieval documents, training labels, routing thresholds, caches, and fallback. Distinguish necessary legality constraints from performance preferences; keeping legality authoritative is desirable, but hiding performance pruning there invalidates attribution.

Use the unchanged production compiler as the quality reference. A deliberately small mechanical selector is an ablation, not the sole baseline. Include a credible compact human policy, a small learned tabular selector, Jev-only if relevant, direct strong-model selection, the proposed cascade, and random/search or Bayesian optimization over the same legal actions. Ansor already combines evolutionary search and a learned cost model; comparison with learning or search is substantive prior-art control. [Ansor paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06762)

All replacement arms should share features, action space, transformation mechanics, acceptance checks, and fallback rules. Compare both equal measurement budgets and actual end-to-end cost frontiers. If the strong model proposes new actions, give search access to them or report action-space expansion as a separate treatment. Retain a best-measured candidate reference to expose headroom, without calling an incomplete search an oracle.

## Minimum decisive validation

1. **Boundary gate.** Trace actual decisions to show the replacement bypasses the named production selector. Report how often downstream policy overrides it and production fallback supplies the accepted result. If production still decides the substantive schedule, narrow the claim to configuration selection or orchestration.
2. **Quality gate.** Predeclare application-derived performance tolerances, compilation latency/resource limits, and numerical correctness contracts. Measure independent application families, shapes, dtypes, layouts, and resource-pressure regimes, including unfavorable cases. Use differential/reference checks plus relevant compiler verification; finite testing does not prove semantic equivalence. Report individual regressions, acceptance failures, fallback frequency, and end-to-end application results as well as aggregate speedups.
3. **Escalation gate.** On held-out cases, obtain paired outcomes for cheap-only, direct-strong, and cascade decisions. Replay the same intermediate states and action budgets to isolate strong-model contribution. Compare the router with random escalation at the same rate and simple uncertainty/feature-based routing. Passing means escalation improves the declared quality–cost tradeoff beyond these controls; additional trials alone do not count as reasoning value. If direct-strong plus caching dominates, reject the cascade architecture. If the cheap model suffices, omit escalation.
4. **Simplification gate.** Compare the complete policy inventory and engineering effort, including labels, prompt edits, debugging, monitoring, and review. Deleted lines are supporting evidence, not the outcome. Passing requires reduced expert maintenance at the agreed quality level through multiple independently selected change episodes. If rule complexity merely moves into action definitions or prompts, reject the simplification claim.
5. **Adaptation gate.** Hold out a genuinely different GPU generation or capability regime and a later optimization-practice change. Run both frozen-policy transfer and maintained-policy adaptation. In the latter, provide every contender the same newly available specifications, examples, measurements, and expert-time opportunity; record consumed resources. Freezing only the production baseline while refreshing the model's context is unfair. Separate changes within an existing action space from changes requiring new transformations or legality support. If such engineering dominates, narrow adaptation claims accordingly.

Split data by originating application/kernel family and provenance, not random near-duplicate shapes. Keep tuning, router calibration, final evaluation, and adaptation episodes separate. Audit retrieval and cache contents for evaluation leakage. Public benchmarks may already be known to pretrained models: include independently authored workloads and temporal holdouts, without claiming that unknown pretraining contamination is eliminated. A tiny diverse corpus can disprove universal claims or establish feasibility; it cannot establish production-wide reliability.

Choose sample size and repetitions from measured variance, independent workload units, and the smallest practically important difference. Predeclare analysis and stopping rules. Report uncertainty with family-level dependence respected; repeated timing of one kernel does not create independent workloads. LLVM explicitly cautions that low noise alone does not eliminate measurement bias. [LLVM benchmarking guidance](https://llvm.org/docs/Benchmarking.html)

## Confidence, economics, and update governance

Jev confidence summarizes its answer distribution; it is not a correctness certificate. Calibrate routing against measured schedule regret and failure risk, including shifted targets. Jev 1.13 documentation flags numeric precision and option-order weaknesses: compute arithmetic in code and test representation/order sensitivity. Do not transfer those version-specific findings or thresholds untested to 1.14. Pin and log exact versions; TypeSafe documents moving aliases. [Confidence](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence), [1.13 limitations](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13), [models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models)

Account separately for research expense, offline policy construction, per-compilation operation, and lifetime maintenance. Include failed candidates, GPU measurements, training, inference, retries, validation, storage, human effort, and service latency/outages. Existing subscription access may reduce immediate cash outlay; it does not establish deployment marginal cost or capacity. With expected reuse count N, compare build/update cost plus N times serving cost under several realistic reuse rates. Report cold-cache, warm-cache, and invalidation cases; no token cap does not mean cost is irrelevant.

Validated outputs should enter a versioned candidate dataset, not silently rewrite deployed policy. Require provenance, target/toolchain fingerprints, independent remeasurement, regression evaluation, explicit promotion, and rollback. An accepted schedule is evidence for that context, not a universally correct training label. Test a distilled offline selector with strong-model access disabled on new workloads: exact cache hits demonstrate reuse; held-out success demonstrates generalization.

Proceed only as separate, falsifiable selector-quality, escalation-value, and maintenance/adaptation hypotheses. Reject any component dominated by a simpler alternative; an inexpensive non-LLM selector would be a useful research result.
